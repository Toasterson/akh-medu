# Language Analysis Procedure

## Analysing a Sentence

To analyse the structure and meaning of a sentence:

1. **Parse sentence structure**: Identify the subject, predicate, verb phrase,
   and object. Note whether the sentence is declarative, interrogative, or
   imperative.

2. **Tag parts of speech**: For each word in the sentence, determine whether it
   is a noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition, conjunction, or
   determiner.

3. **Resolve anaphora**: For any pronoun or definite noun phrase, find the noun
   or noun phrase it refers back to in the prior context.

## Quick Part-of-Speech Tagging

For a single word in context, identify its part of speech and note any
morphological features (tense, number, case).

## Understanding Discourse Relations

To understand how two clauses or sentences are related:

1. Parse each clause for its structural components.
2. Determine the discourse relation: contrast, cause-effect, elaboration,
   exemplification, temporal ordering, or condition.

## Important Principles

- Always consider context when tagging: "light" is an adjective in "light rain"
  but a noun in "turn off the light."
- Pronouns are resolved using the most recent, gender-congruent antecedent.
- Discourse relations are often signalled by connectives ("however", "because",
  "for example") but may be implicit.
